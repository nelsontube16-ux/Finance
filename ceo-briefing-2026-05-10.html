IHSG dropped from 7,594 (Apr 20) to 7,171 — a significant decline while US markets hit records. Indonesia is caught in a triple squeeze: (1) MSCI downgrade threat, (2) regulatory upheaval from free float reforms, (3) oil import costs from Hormuz. The market is decoupling from the global rally. Underperforming Triple Squeeze

🚫 MSCI Threatens Frontier Downgrade NEW CRITICAL

MSCI has frozen rebalancing of Indonesian stock indices for May 2026 and warned of a potential downgrade from Emerging Market to Frontier Market unless Indonesia makes massive transparency and free float improvements. This would be catastrophic: EM-to-Frontier downgrades historically trigger 20–30% outflows from passive funds. OJK (the regulator) called the freeze "positive" — but the market disagrees. This is Indonesia's $80 billion wake-up call (The Diplomat).

Downgrade Risk MSCI Freeze NEW SINCE APR 26

📜 Free Float Doubled to 15% — Index Purge Begins

On March 31, OJK doubled the required free float from 7.5% to 15% (phased by market cap). Effective May 1, the IDX has banned stocks with high ownership concentration from IDX30, LQ45, and IDX80. This is a structural cleansing of Indonesia's indices — painful short-term (forced selling, index disruption) but positive long-term (better governance, deeper liquidity). Companies failing to meet thresholds face potential delisting.

Reform Forced Selling Long-Term Positive

📊 IPO Pipeline: Frozen but 50-Target Still Stands

IDX maintains its 50-IPO target for 2026 (revised down from 66), with at least 6 "lighthouse" large-cap IPOs expected. However, early 2026 delivered near-zero listings. The free float reforms create a paradox: raising listing standards makes new IPOs harder in the short term but more attractive to international investors long-term. The DEWA/Gayo Mineral Resources IPO (gold & copper, Bakrie Group) remains under internal study with no final decision. Watch for a H2 2026 IPO window if MSCI concerns are addressed.

🌐 Macro — what equity research has to price this week

Three macro vectors set the discount rate and demand backdrop for every name you'd pitch. Track these — not headlines about them — because they move multiples directly.

1. Real rates & the Fed path

The Fed funds rate sets the risk-free leg of your WACC. Mechanism: WACC = Cost of Equity × E/V + Cost of Debt × (1−t) × D/V; Cost of Equity ≈ Rf + β × ERP. A 25 bp move in the 10Y yield mechanically shifts a typical equity target ±2–4% via terminal-value discounting. Watch: next FOMC dot-plot, PCE inflation print, 2s10s spread (steepening = recession risk fading; inversion = trouble).

2. Dollar (DXY) & EM currencies

Mechanism for IDR: a stronger USD pulls capital out of Indonesia → BI defends rupiah → tightens liquidity → IHSG multiples compress. Conversely, a weaker DXY is rocket fuel for Indonesian banks and consumer names. Watch: DXY level, USD/IDR vs the 15,500–16,500 band, Bank Indonesia's BI Rate decision.

3. Commodities (oil, coal, nickel, CPO)

Indonesia is uniquely commodity-levered. Read-through: Coal price ↑ → ADRO / ITMG / PTBA earnings ↑. Nickel ↑ → INCO / ANTM / Harita. CPO ↑ → AALI / LSIP / SIMP. Brent ↑ → MEDC, but margin squeeze for AKR fuel-distribution and PTBA's diesel costs. Always model these as sensitivity tables, never point estimates.

Catalyst calendar — next 48h

US: PCE inflation print, Univ. of Michigan sentiment final. EU: PMI flash readings. Asia: BoJ minutes, China LPR fix. Indonesia: BI rate decision (monthly), trade balance.

What to do with it

Build a one-page "macro dashboard" tab in every pitch deck: Fed funds, 10Y, DXY, Brent, IDR, IHSG vs MSCI EM. Refresh weekly. This is what MDs ask in the first 30 seconds of any review.

📈 Global markets — framework for the open

Saturday — US, Europe, and most Asian cash markets are closed. Use the weekend to do the work that pays off Monday: revisit your thesis, refresh comps, and pre-write the Monday morning note.

What moves at Monday open

  • Gap risk — any weekend headline (geopolitics, M&A, central-bank speak) is priced in the first 15 minutes. Identify the names with the most weekend "news beta" and have a view ready.
  • Friday closing tape — re-read the SPX / NDX / STOXX600 closes and breadth (advance/decline). Narrow breadth + index near highs = fragile rally; wide breadth = healthier.
  • VIX vs realized vol — if VIX trades at a premium to 30-day realized, options are overpriced (sell-vol bias). At a discount, the market is complacent (buy-protection bias).

Sector rotation watch

In a "higher-for-longer" rate regime, the rotation pattern that has rewarded analysts is: quality + cash-generative (large-cap industrials, defensive consumer staples) over long-duration unprofitable growth. If rates start falling, flip the trade: small-cap, biotech, unprofitable tech outperform on duration. Position your stock pitch with explicit "if rates do X, my call still works because Y."

Quick mechanism — why M&A premium matters for your pitch

When you write "trading at 8.5× EV/EBITDA vs peer median 11×," the immediate counter is "why doesn't it close the gap?" The answer is often strategic optionality — i.e., who could acquire it, at what premium. A clean stock pitch lists 2–3 plausible acquirers and a back-of-envelope take-out price: Take-out EV ≈ Standalone EV + Synergy NPV × (1 − sponsor share). Even if you don't bank on the deal, the floor it implies tightens your downside.

🌍 Geopolitics — how to translate headlines into a discount rate

Most analysts read geopolitics for color. Good analysts price it. Three transmission channels:

  • Energy supply — Middle East, Russia/Ukraine, Strait of Hormuz, Red Sea shipping. Direct hit on Brent → input cost for ~40% of S&P sectors, plus airline / chemicals margin.
  • Trade & tariffs — US–China decoupling, EU CBAM, Indonesia nickel export rules. Affects semis, autos, batteries, and Indonesian downstream metals.
  • Election & policy risk — fiscal policy shifts → bond yields → equity multiples. Track US election cycle, ASEAN summits, BI / OJK policy speeches.

The formula MDs respect: "Headline X widens the equity risk premium by ~25–50 bp; my fair value moves from Rp Y to Rp Z; I am still a Buy because Z is still > current price by N%." Don't say "uncertain"; quantify.

📚 Book of the day — read 15 minutes daily

If you only have time for one book to live in your bag through internship season, this is it. Why: it teaches you the language of valuation, the workflow of an analyst, and the structure of a pitch in one volume — the same book taught in JP Morgan, MS, and GS associate training.

Investment Banking — Rosenbaum & Pearl

Investment Banking: Valuation, LBOs, M&A, and IPOs

Joshua Rosenbaum & Joshua Pearl — Wiley Finance, 3rd edition.

Why this book first: it walks you through the four pillars of IB work — Comparable Companies, Precedent Transactions, DCF, and LBO — in exactly the order MDs expect you to think. Each chapter has the full worked example (Valor Acquisition Corp. case), templates, and football-field output. Spend 15 minutes a day, and in 6 weeks you'll be functionally fluent.

15-minute daily reading plan (6 weeks)

  • Week 1: Chapter 1 — Comparable Companies Analysis. Build one comps table by hand.
  • Week 2: Chapter 2 — Precedent Transactions. Pull 5 deals from CapIQ & replicate.
  • Week 3: Chapter 3 — DCF, part 1 (forecasting revenue, EBIT, FCF).
  • Week 4: Chapter 3 — DCF, part 2 (WACC, terminal value, sensitivities).
  • Week 5: Chapter 4 — LBO, part 1 (sources & uses, debt schedule).
  • Week 6: Chapter 4–5 — LBO returns & full M&A process.

Once you've read it, rotate into:

  • Damodaran on Valuation — for the theory behind the practice.
  • Barbarians at the Gate (Burrough & Helyar) — the canonical PE / LBO story; required cultural reading.
  • The Intelligent Investor (Graham) — for the equity-pitch mindset (margin of safety).
  • Margin of Safety (Klarman) — out-of-print but PDFs circulate; pure value-investing logic.

🧠 Knowledge feed — one concept, fully explained

Each brief teaches one concept end-to-end. Today: the single most-tested concept in IB interviews and the spine of every pitch.

Enterprise Value (EV) and why it isn't market cap
noun · /ˈentərˌprīz ˈvalyo͞o/ · "the price tag of the business"

The formula

EV = Equity value + Debt − Cash + Preferred + Minority Interest

Why it exists

Imagine two identical pizzerias making the same Rp 10 bn EBITDA. Pizzeria A has Rp 0 debt and Rp 5 bn cash. Pizzeria B has Rp 5 bn debt and Rp 0 cash. If both have a Rp 100 bn market cap, are they equally expensive?

No. If you bought all the equity of A, you'd inherit Rp 5 bn cash — your real outlay is Rp 95 bn. Buy B and you inherit Rp 5 bn of debt — your real outlay is Rp 105 bn. That real outlay is Enterprise Value. It's what you'd pay to own the whole business free of its capital structure.

Why analysts pair EV with EBITDA (not P/E)

EBITDA sits above interest expense, so it's also capital-structure-neutral. Matching EV / EBITDA gives you an apples-to-apples multiple across firms with different debt loads. P/E is contaminated by leverage — a highly levered firm shows a lower P/E that has nothing to do with operational quality.

The interview trick they love

"A company has $1 bn market cap, $200 m debt, $100 m cash. What's EV?" → $1.1 bn. Then: "If it issues $300 m of new debt and holds the cash, what happens to EV?" → Nothing. Cash up $300 m, debt up $300 m, they cancel. EV is invariant to capital-structure changes. That's the whole point of EV.

Where this shows up in your stock pitch

Every football-field chart in every pitch deck is in EV / EBITDA or EV / Revenue. When you say "the market is mispricing this by 25%," you mean the implied EV. Equity-value-per-share is the output; EV is the input. Master EV → equity bridge and you're 80% of the way to looking like an analyst, not a student.

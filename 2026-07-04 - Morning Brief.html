🎓 Finance Student Knowledge Feed — Concept of the Day

What is a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF), and why does every IB analyst live inside one?

A DCF is the tool bankers use to answer "what is this company actually worth today?" — not what the market says, but what the future cash it generates is worth right now. It's the foundation of every pitch, every fairness opinion, every M&A decision. Skipping DCF fluency = not making it past first-round intern interviews.

The core intuition (in one sentence)

Money in the future is worth less than money today, because (a) you could invest today's money and earn a return, and (b) the future is uncertain. So we discount future cash flows back to a "present value."

The 5 steps every DCF has

1. Project Free Cash Flow (FCF) for 5–10 years. FCF = EBIT × (1 − tax rate) + D&A − CapEx − ΔWorking Capital. This is the cash the business generates after paying to keep itself alive.
2. Estimate a Terminal Value (TV) — what the business is worth at the end of the projection period. Two methods: Gordon Growth (assume FCF grows forever at ~2–3%) or Exit Multiple (assume it's sold for X× EBITDA).
3. Calculate WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) — the blended rate at which we discount. WACC = (E/V × Re) + (D/V × Rd × (1−T)). This is the "opportunity cost" — what investors expect to earn given the risk.
4. Discount everything back: PV = FCF ÷ (1 + WACC)^n. Add up all present values of FCFs + present value of TV = Enterprise Value.
5. Bridge EV → Equity Value: Equity Value = EV − Net Debt − Minority Interest + Cash. Divide by shares outstanding = intrinsic price per share. Compare vs. market price → buy / hold / sell.

Where interns get destroyed in DCF interviews

  • "Why do we use WACC, not just cost of equity?" → Because we're valuing the entire enterprise (debt + equity), not just the shareholders' slice. FCF is pre-financing.
  • "What's the sensitivity of your DCF?" → 90% of the value in a typical DCF is in the Terminal Value. A 0.5% change in WACC or perpetuity growth swings valuation by 15–25%. This is why DCF gets called "garbage in, gospel out."
  • "When is DCF the wrong tool?" → Early-stage / negative-FCF companies (pre-2020 tech), banks (use P/TBV instead), REITs (use FFO). DCF loves stable, predictable cash generators.

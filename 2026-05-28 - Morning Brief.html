Knowledge Feed — For Your Internship

Study

Four things worth knowing cold before you walk onto a desk — each tied to something that actually happened this week, so it sticks.

Concept · FX & Central Banking

The Impossible Trinity (why BI had to hike)

A central bank cannot simultaneously have all three of: (1) a stable exchange rate, (2) free capital flows, and (3) independent monetary policy. You can only pick two. When the Rupiah was collapsing and capital was flowing out freely, Bank Indonesia had to sacrifice "independent policy" — it couldn't cut to support growth; it was forced to hike (to 5.25%) to defend the currency, even into a slowdown.

This is also why FX rose on the hike while stocks fell: higher rates reward holding the currency (good for IDR) but raise the cost of capital and slow growth (bad for equities).

Live example: BI's surprise 50bp hike, May 20, 2026 — currency up, IHSG down the same day.

Concept · Inflation Data

PCE vs CPI — and why the Fed prefers PCE

Both measure inflation, but the Fed targets core PCE (Personal Consumption Expenditures, ex-food & energy). PCE updates its "basket" as people substitute (buy chicken when beef gets dear), covers a broader scope (incl. employer-paid healthcare), and so usually runs a touch below CPI. "Core" strips out volatile food/energy to see the underlying trend.

Key skill: read the YoY vs MoM split. This week headline PCE was 3.8% YoY (scary) but only +0.4% MoM and decelerating (reassuring) — markets traded the direction at the margin, not the level. Always check the monthly run-rate, not just the annual headline.

Live example: April PCE released May 28, 2026 — hot annual, soft monthly, stocks rallied.

Concept · Equity Capital Markets

IPO mechanics — "pop", greenshoe, lock-up

When Cerebras priced at $185 and opened at $350, that ~+68% "pop" is the gap between the price the bank set and where the market clears. A big pop means money was "left on the table" for the issuer — great for day-one buyers, debatable for the company. Two terms you'll be asked about:

Greenshoe (over-allotment): lets underwriters sell up to ~15% extra shares to stabilise the price. Lock-up: insiders typically can't sell for ~90–180 days post-IPO — so watch the lock-up expiry as a supply event that often pressures the stock.

Live example: Cerebras (CBRS) IPO, May 14, 2026 — biggest tech IPO since Snowflake; SpaceX S-1 filed May 20.

Playbook · Equity Research

How to structure an Equity Research stock pitch

The standard skeleton an analyst (and your interviewer) expects:

  1. Recommendation up top: Buy/Sell, price target, % up/down side, time horizon. The conclusion goes first.
  2. The thesis in 2–3 points: what the market is missing (the "variant perception"). No pitch without an edge.
  3. Business & drivers: how the company makes money and the 2–3 KPIs that actually move the stock.
  4. Valuation: DCF and comps (EV/EBITDA, P/E vs peers). Show the bridge from assumptions to target.
  5. Catalysts: what makes the gap close, and when (earnings, deal, product, macro).
  6. Risks & the bear case: steelman the other side, then say why you're still right. This is what separates good from average.
  7. Scenario range: bull / base / bear targets — never a single point estimate (especially with oil this headline-driven).

Apply it: pick one name from this brief, run this skeleton, and you have a first draft.

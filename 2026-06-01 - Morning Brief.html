10 🎓 Today's Finance Lesson

One concept per day. No prior background assumed.

Today's concept

Net Interest Margin (NIM) — why BI's hike is good for BBRI

A bank makes money on the spread between what it earns on assets (loans, bonds) and what it pays on liabilities (deposits, borrowings). That spread, expressed as a percent of average earning assets, is the Net Interest Margin.

NIM = (Interest Income − Interest Expense) / Average Earning Assets

Why it matters today: when a central bank raises rates, banks reprice their loans (assets) almost immediately because most corporate loans float on a benchmark. But banks reprice deposits (liabilities) much more slowly — savers don't demand higher rates overnight. That gap = expanding NIM = expanding earnings. This is called being "asset-sensitive."

Indonesian big banks (BBRI, BMRI) are asset-sensitive. So BI's surprise +50bp hike yesterday is, mechanically, a 2–3 quarter tailwind to NIM — even though the macro headlines look scary. This is exactly the kind of "headline says bad, structure says good" angle a pitch should exploit.

Vocabulary you should now own:

  • Asset-sensitive bank — earnings rise when rates rise.
  • Liability-sensitive bank — earnings fall when rates rise (e.g. CASA-light digital banks).
  • CASA ratio — Current Account / Savings Account share of deposits. High CASA = cheap funding = wider NIM.
  • Loan-to-Deposit Ratio (LDR) — bank's lending capacity vs. funding. BI watches this closely.

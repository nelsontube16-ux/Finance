🌍 Macro & Geopolitics

~2 min
FEDFOMC held at 3.50–3.75%, but the dot plot flipped from cut → hike

Fourth straight hold on June 17. The body of the statement is unchanged, but the SEP (Summary of Economic Projections) is the news: PCE inflation revised up to 3.6% (from 2.7%), 2027 PCE to 3.3% (from 2.7%), 2026 GDP cut to 2.2% (from 2.4%). The median dot no longer shows a 2026 cut; 9 of 19 officials now see at least one hike, 6 see two.

Mechanism for a beginner: the "dot plot" is a chart where each FOMC voter anonymously plots where they think rates will be at year-end. It's not a vote on policy — it's a survey of forecasts. When the median dot moves up, traders re-price the front end of the yield curve, the dollar strengthens, and growth equity (long-duration cash flows) gets penalized. That's the chain of causality you're watching.

🇮🇩 Indonesia Deep Dive

~2 min
BISecond hike in nine days — BI-Rate at 5.75%, +100 bp since May

Bank Indonesia delivered an off-cycle 25 bp hike on 9 June, then another 25 bp at the regular meeting 18 June. Cumulative tightening since May: +100 bp, the highest policy rate since April 2025. The trigger is two-fold: rupiah depreciation (driven by foreign portfolio outflows and onshore FX demand) and inflation drifting to 3.08% in May from 2.42% in April — closing in on the upper bound of the 1.5–3.5% target. GDP growth forecast maintained at 4.9–5.7% for 2026.

Beginner mechanism: when domestic rates rise faster than US rates, the carry on holding IDR improves — that should stem outflows and stabilize the rupiah. But the bank channel pays the bill: BBRI, BMRI, BBCA's NIMs (net interest margins) get squeezed because deposit costs rise faster than they can re-price loans. This is why ID banks were in the selling Friday.
IHSG−3.8% on the week to ~5,918 — MSCI frontier-downgrade tail risk

First weekly loss in three. Broad-based selling Friday hit all major sectors. The structural overhang is the MSCI review: a potential frontier-market downgrade was delayed to November but not removed. Foreign positioning is light, which is both a relief (less to dump) and a risk (less buying power).

Watchlist for the open: banks (BBRI, BMRI, BBCA, BBNI) for the NIM trade; consumer staples (INDF, ICBP, UNVR) as defensives if the rupiah stabilizes; energy (MEDC, PGAS, ADRO) on the Qatar LNG read-across; and Telkom (TLKM) as a yield proxy if foreign flows return.

🎓 Today's Lesson · Finance 101

~3 min

Three concepts that come up every day on an IB / PE / M&A desk. Learn them, and you will follow 80% of the conversation in your first month.

Net Interest Margin (NIM)

N-I-M · banks

The spread between what a bank earns on its loans and what it pays on its deposits, expressed as a % of average earning assets. It is the single most important profitability metric for a commercial bank — like gross margin is for a manufacturer.

Today's link: Bank Indonesia's +100 bp hike since May squeezes BBRI/BMRI NIM because their deposit costs reset faster than their loan books. This is exactly why ID banks led Friday's sell-off — analysts are cutting FY26 NIM forecasts by 10–20 bps.

EV / EBITDA

E-V-EBITDA · the universal comp

Enterprise Value (market cap + debt − cash) divided by EBITDA. Used in M&A because it is capital-structure-neutral — you compare two businesses regardless of how they're financed. P/E doesn't work for that because it's after interest & tax.

Today's link: Safran is reportedly paying ~12–13× EV/EBITDA for Exail — rich for defense, but justified by the autonomous-naval IP. When you read about a deal, the first number to look up is the EV/EBITDA multiple paid vs. the sector median.

The Dot Plot

F-O-M-C · the Fed's tea leaves

Each FOMC voting member anonymously marks where they think the federal funds rate should be at the end of each future year. The chart of those dots = the dot plot. The median dot is the headline number traders react to.

Today's link: Friday's dot plot moved the median from a 2026 cut to no cut, with 9/19 members penciling in a hike. That's why the 2-year Treasury sold off and the dollar firmed. Long-duration assets (growth tech) pay for it via a higher discount rate.

The IPO "Window"

I-P-O · syndication shorthand

When market conditions are favorable enough that issuers can price deals at full valuation, bankers say "the window is open." A window closes when 2–3 recent IPOs break issue price in their first 5–10 trading days — buyers refuse to participate, and pipeline gets pushed.

Today's link: SpaceX broke issue inside 10 days. OpenAI just punted to 2027. If ITG and Sinda break issue this week, the Q3 window is functionally shut and your IB deal-pipeline conversations next quarter will sound very different.

Rosenbaum & Pearl

Investment Banking: Valuation, LBOs, M&A, and IPOs

Joshua Rosenbaum & Joshua Pearl · 3rd Ed.

This is the industry-standard textbook that first-year IB analysts at Goldman, Morgan Stanley, and JP Morgan are handed on day one. It is the only book that walks you end-to-end through the four pillars of the job: (1) comparable company analysis, (2) precedent transactions, (3) DCF, and (4) LBO modeling — with the same templates you'll build in Excel at work.

For a finance student with limited time, the protocol is: 15–20 pages every morning, 4 days a week. The chapters are self-contained, so you can stop after one mechanism (e.g., "calculating LTM EBITDA") and apply it the same day to a real ticker.

Why this and not something else? Other books (Damodaran, Greenblatt, Lewis) are excellent for theory, narrative, or buy-side intuition. Rosenbaum & Pearl is the only one whose chapter structure mirrors the deliverables in an IB internship. Read it now, and the first 6 weeks of your internship will feel like review.

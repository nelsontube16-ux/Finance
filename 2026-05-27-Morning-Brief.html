LESSON OF THE DAY
▲ TODAY'S CONCEPT

The "Bull Steepener" — and why it's the trade hiding in plain sight

Yesterday the 2Y dropped 7bps to 4.06% and the 30Y dropped 7bps to 5.01%. Both ends rallied (yields fell). When the short end falls faster than the long end, you get a bull steepener. When both fall together you get a bull flattener or parallel rally — yesterday was close to parallel, but the directional signal still matters.

Yield curve slope = Y(long) − Y(short)   |   e.g., 30Y − 2Y = 5.01 − 4.06 = +95bps

WHY IT MATTERS

A bull steepener typically signals the market believes the Fed will cut soon (front end rallies hardest) while long-end inflation expectations stay anchored or sticky. It's the textbook "growth scare" trade. A bear steepener (long end sells off harder) signals inflation fear.

HOW TO EXPRESS IT

You don't need exotic instruments: long IEF / TLT (long duration ETFs), or long SHY + short TLT for a pure-curve view. On the institutional side: 2s10s steepener via futures (long ZT, short ZN, beta-weighted).

FOR YOUR PITCH

If you're writing equity research, this matters because a steepening curve historically supports banks (NIM expansion) and cyclicals, and pressures long-duration growth (software, biotech). A flattening curve does the opposite.

WHAT TO REMEMBER

Memorize the 4 quadrants: Bull Steepener = growth scare / cuts coming · Bear Steepener = inflation fear · Bull Flattener = soft landing · Bear Flattener = Fed hawkish / late-cycle. This single 2×2 is >50% of fixed-income desk conversation.

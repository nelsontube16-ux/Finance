Mechanism Explainer · for someone with no background

Why does a CPI print move stocks? When inflation runs higher than the central bank wants, the bank either keeps rates high or hikes them. Higher rates = (1) the "risk-free rate" investors compare every stock to goes up, so future earnings get discounted more heavily — i.e. a $1 of profit 5 years from now is worth less today; (2) borrowing costs rise for both companies (margins squeezed) and consumers (less spending). Long-duration growth stocks (tech, biotech) are most sensitive because most of their value is in future profits. Value/short-duration stocks (banks, energy, staples) are less sensitive or can even benefit.

13 Today's New Knowledge

For your IB/PE internship prep · explained from zero background
Concept · 01

Enterprise Value vs Equity Value

Pick this up first — it's the single most-tested IB interview topic.

Equity Value (Market Cap) = share price × shares outstanding. This is what shareholders own.

Enterprise Value (EV) = Equity Value + Debt − Cash + Preferred + Minority Interest. This is the cost to buy the whole company outright (you'd have to pay off the debt but you get the cash).

Why both? When comparing companies, EV is "capital-structure neutral" — it doesn't matter whether the company is financed with debt or equity. That's why valuation multiples like EV/EBITDA use EV, not market cap. P/E uses Equity (because EPS is already after interest expense).

Worked example: Company has 100M shares × $20 = $2B equity. $500M debt, $100M cash, no preferred/minority. EV = 2,000 + 500 − 100 = $2,400M. If EBITDA = $200M, EV/EBITDA = 12.0x.

Concept · 02

The DCF in one paragraph

A Discounted Cash Flow says: a company's value today = the sum of all the cash it will generate in the future, "discounted" back to today using a rate that reflects how risky those cash flows are.

Three pieces: (1) Project Free Cash Flow (FCF) for 5–10 years; (2) Calculate a Terminal Value (the value of all years after your forecast, usually via Gordon Growth: TV = FCF × (1+g) / (WACC − g)); (3) Discount everything to PV using WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital).

Why bankers care: Every fairness opinion, every pitch, every sell-side process produces a "football field" valuation chart with DCF as one of the 4–5 bars.

Concept · 03 · today's market lens

Why does the Fed hold matter for DCFs?

WACC has a risk-free rate input — usually the 10-year Treasury yield. When the Fed signals "higher for longer" (today's story), the 10Y stays elevated, WACC stays elevated, and every DCF in every pitch becomes lower. That's the mechanical link between "Fed dot plot" and "stock prices fall". When a senior asks you why tech sold off on a CPI print — this is the answer.

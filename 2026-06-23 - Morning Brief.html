Mechanism · why oil & gold are moving opposite directions
Oil reflects supply-shock risk premium — Hormuz carries ~20% of seaborne oil, so closure spikes prices and reopening crushes them. Gold reflects real interest rates + currency debasement hedge. Even as the war risk fades, sticky inflation (CPI 4.2%) and Fed-pause uncertainty keep real rates compressed → gold stays bid. This is the classic geopolitical risk vs. monetary regime divergence — useful in any macro pitch.

Mechanism · why EM central banks hike when the Fed pauses
When the Fed holds at high rates, the USD interest-rate differential with EM currencies stays wide. Capital flows to USD assets → EM currencies depreciate → imported inflation rises → EM central banks must hike more than the Fed to keep their currency stable. This is the "impossible trinity": a country can have only 2 of {open capital account, fixed FX, independent monetary policy}. BI is choosing FX stability over growth.

Mechanism: Most global EM funds (passive + active benchmarked) are forbidden from holding frontier-market equities. A downgrade triggers forced selling regardless of fundamentals. Estimated outflow: USD 8–12 billion over 60–90 days.

Most exposed Indonesian names: BBCABBRIBMRITLKMASII — these dominate EM-Indonesia ETFs. A downgrade = mechanical selling pressure on top blue-chips.

Pitch angle — why "secondary buyouts" are surging
Triton buying Flender from Carlyle is a secondary buyout (PE → PE). Happens when: (1) seller's fund is near end-of-life, (2) buyer sees operational upside the seller exhausted, or (3) public-market exits are frozen (IPO window narrow). Currently it's mostly (1) + (3): 2020–22 vintage funds need DPI returns to LPs and the IPO market doesn't clear the price. Implication for your pitch: sponsor-backed companies are net sellers — look for businesses owned by 2018–2020 funds that haven't exited (use PitchBook / Preqin).

12Finance 101 · Today's Lesson

How to structure an Equity Research report

You'll be asked to write one in your internship. Memorize this skeleton — every sell-side note follows it.

The 7-Section Equity Research Template

This is the same structure used in Goldman, MS, and CLSA notes. Master it once, reuse forever.

1 · Tagline
One sentence + recommendation + price target. Example: "BBCA: Initiate at BUY, TP IDR 12,500 (+18%) — NIM expansion under BI hike cycle is underpriced by 8% vs. consensus."
2 · Investment Thesis
3 bullets max. Why this stock, why now, why not consensus. Each bullet ties to a number.
3 · Catalysts
Time-bound triggers over next 3–12 months: earnings, regulatory, product launch, refi event. Without catalysts, your call is "value trap risk."
4 · Financial Model
3-statement model (IS, BS, CF) projected 5 years, plus a separate operating drivers tab (volume × price × margin). The model is the work.
5 · Valuation
Triangulate 3 methods: DCF (intrinsic) + Comps (relative) + Precedents (M&A floor). Then footnote your TP weighting (e.g., "60% DCF / 30% Comps / 10% Precedent"). TP = wₐ · V_DCF + w_b · V_Comp + w_c · V_M&A
6 · Risks
3–5 risks, ranked, each with impact in $/share. "Macro slowdown" is not a risk — "100 bps BI hike reduces FY27 EPS by IDR 240, –6% to TP" is.
7 · Appendix
Comp table, DCF sensitivity (WACC × g), assumption page. PMs flip here first to test if your model breaks.
Mechanism · why analysts always Buy more than they Sell
Sell-side ratings distribute roughly 50% Buy / 40% Hold / 10% Sell. Reason: investment-banking conflicts (a Sell rating burns the corporate-access relationship) + career risk (a Buy that fails is "the company missed"; a Sell that fails is "you were wrong"). Adjust mentally — a "Hold" is often a real Sell.

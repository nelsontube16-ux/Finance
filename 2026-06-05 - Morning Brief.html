The Big Story

What's moving markets
Chip stocks just had their worst day since April '25 — and it wasn't earnings.
On Friday Jun 5, the Nasdaq dropped 4.18% to 25,709 — its biggest single-day loss in 14 months. S&P 500 fell 2.64%; Dow off 695 pts (-1.35%). Roughly $1 trillion in market cap evaporated from semiconductors alone.

What triggered it: Two things collided.

  1. Broadcom (AVGO) guidance miss. Strong earnings, but AI-semiconductor revenue guide came in below Street estimates. This is the company that's been the cleanest "AI infrastructure" proxy after Nvidia — a soft guide there reprices the whole AI buildout thesis.
  2. Hot May jobs print (172K vs. 80K expected). 10-yr Treasury yield jumped above 4.5%, 30-yr above 5%. Higher yields = higher discount rate = lower present value of long-duration growth stocks (semis, software, AI).
↳ Mechanism (read if you don't have the background)

Why do yields matter for tech? A stock's price ≈ the sum of its future cash flows, discounted back to today. The discount rate moves with the 10-yr Treasury yield. Tech firms earn most of their cash far in the future ("long duration"). When yields rise from 4.0% → 4.5%, every dollar of future profit is worth less today. Mathematically: a small move in r causes a big move in P for high-duration assets. That's why semis/tech got hit hardest while value stocks held up.

Why the jobs number hurt: A hot labor market = wage pressure = sticky inflation = Fed stays restrictive longer = yields rise. The market wanted a soft landing; the print said "no landing yet."

Indonesia · IHSG

This is the headline you can't miss
IHSG down 4.8% Thursday — now the worst-performing major index globally YTD.
IHSG fell 283 pts to ~5,653 on Thursday Jun 4, extending a brutal seventh consecutive weekly decline (-6% on the week, -33% YTD). Worse than any of the ~90 major global indices tracked.

The three things killing the index

  1. Foreign outflows: Net foreign sell > IDR 67 trillion YTD. Foreigners are leaving Indonesian equities in volume — not just trimming.
  2. MSCI downgrade risk: Speculation that Indonesia could be cut to Frontier Market status at the Jun 19 review. If that happens, every passive EM index fund has to sell mechanically — forced selling on top of foreign exit.
  3. Sticky inflation: May CPI ran 3.08%, above BI's target midpoint. Limits Bank Indonesia's room to cut rates & defend growth.
↳ What "MSCI downgrade to Frontier" actually means (mechanism)

MSCI classifies countries as Developed → Emerging → Frontier → Standalone. Trillions of dollars of passive index funds (ETFs, pension allocations) track MSCI EM. If Indonesia is downgraded to Frontier, those funds must sell Indonesian holdings, by mandate, regardless of fundamentals. The downgrade itself is the catalyst — markets pre-position by selling now in anticipation. This is why headline risk > fundamentals right now.

Why might they downgrade? Liquidity concerns, capital-market accessibility, FX convertibility. The new commodity export rules (via PT DSI) flagged earlier this month likely contributed to "accessibility concerns" in MSCI's framework.

↳ Why the US 10-yr yield is the single most important number on your screen

The US 10-yr Treasury yield is the "risk-free rate" used in nearly every valuation model on Wall Street. DCF discount rates, equity risk premia, mortgage rates, EM credit spreads, FX carry trades — all anchor to it. When it moves, everything moves.

For Indonesia specifically: when US 10-yr rises, the IDR/USD interest-rate differential narrows, capital flows out of EM into US Treasuries. That's literally what's happening in the IDR 67T foreign-outflow number above.

Finance Crash-Course

One concept / day
📚 Today's concept — Duration Risk

What it is: "Duration" measures how sensitive an asset's price is to a change in interest rates. It comes from bonds (a 10-yr Treasury has duration of ~9 yrs — meaning a 1% rise in yield drops its price ~9%). But the concept extends to everything.

Why it explains today's tape:

  • Tech stocks have "long duration" because most of their cash flows are 5–20 years out. When yields rise, they fall hard.
  • Banks & cyclicals have "short duration" — they earn now. They're less hurt (sometimes helped) by rising yields.
  • The Russell 2000 (small caps) rose while the Nasdaq dropped 4% — same yield move, opposite duration profile.

How to use it in an interview: "I'd frame the chip selloff as a duration repricing, not a fundamentals story. AVGO's miss was the trigger but not the cause — the cause is the 10-yr going from 4.0 to 4.5%."

That single sentence will sound 2 years more experienced than your peers.

Mini-glossary (taken from today's brief)

  • NFP — Nonfarm Payrolls. Monthly US jobs report. Most-watched US data print after CPI.
  • Dot plot — FOMC's chart of where each member sees rates going. "Hawkish drift" = the median dot moves up.
  • Book build — Pre-IPO process where underwriters poll institutional demand to set the offer price.
  • Secondary share issuance — Company sells new shares post-IPO. Dilutive to existing holders unless the cash raised earns > cost of equity.
  • NIM — Net Interest Margin. The spread between what banks lend at and pay depositors. Core bank earnings driver.

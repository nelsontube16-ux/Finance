  • Explicit BI mandate right now: rupiah stability first. Macroprudential & payment-system policy stays supportive to keep credit flowing.
Mechanism · Why a rate hike defends a currency

When BI raises rates, IDR-denominated bonds and deposits pay more. Foreign portfolio investors sell USD to buy IDR to earn that yield. That extra USD-selling / IDR-buying pushes the exchange rate stronger (or at least stops it from falling). The cost: domestic borrowing gets more expensive, which slows the real economy. Every central bank hike is this trade-off — currency vs. growth.

Mechanism · Why "equalization payment"?

In a 50/50 JV, both parents are supposed to walk in with equal economic value. If Party A's contributed business is worth more than Party B's on day one, Party B "tops up" the difference in cash — the equalization payment. In the BT/Verizon JV, BT's international enterprise business was worth more than Verizon's; Verizon pays $625M so that both sides start at parity. You'll see this exact structure in almost every JV pitchbook.

🧠 Finance 101 — Today's Concept

Building your intern-ready toolkit. Today's concept is directly relevant to Netflix / Warner Bros. — the biggest live deal on the tape.

Accretion / Dilution Analysis — the M&A intern's first Excel model

The question it answers: If Acquirer buys Target, does Acquirer's earnings per share (EPS) go up (accretive) or down (dilutive)? That single number often decides whether a deal gets pitched, approved, or walked away from.

The three ingredients

  1. Consideration mix — how the acquirer pays.
    • Cash — usually funded with new debt. Adds interest expense.
    • Stock — issue new shares. Adds shares to the denominator.
    • Mix — most real deals (e.g. Netflix/WBD is expected part-stock, part-cash).
  2. Synergies — expected cost savings (headcount, IT, real estate) and revenue lift. Only cost synergies are underwritten in the base case; revenue synergies are a "credit-if-they-come" upside.
  3. Financing cost — after-tax interest on new debt raised.

The math, stripped down

Pro Forma Net Income = Acq NI + Tgt NI + Synergies × (1 − tax) − New Interest × (1 − tax)
Pro Forma Shares = Acq Shares + New Shares Issued
Pro Forma EPS = Pro Forma NI ÷ Pro Forma Shares
Accretion/Dilution = (Pro Forma EPS ÷ Standalone Acq EPS) − 1

The "rule of thumb" everyone quotes

A deal is accretive when the after-tax cost of financing is less than the earnings yield of the target (E/P = inverse of P/E). If Netflix's cost of debt is ~5% after tax and it's buying WBD at a P/E of 15× (earnings yield ~6.7%), the cash portion of the deal is naturally accretive before synergies even come in.

Why interns get asked this

Accretion / dilution is the fastest sanity-check on whether a deal makes sense at first glance. If your MD walks by and asks "is this accretive?" — they want a one-sentence answer inside 30 seconds. Build one of these on paper for the Netflix / WBD deal this weekend. That single exercise will teach you more about M&A than a whole textbook chapter.

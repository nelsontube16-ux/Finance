Mechanism — sanctions blocking statutesA blocking statute makes it illegal for a domestic company to comply with a foreign country's sanction. The targeted firm faces a "damned if you do, damned if you don't" choice: violate US OFAC and lose access to USD clearing, or violate Chinese law and lose Chinese assets. Outcome: corporate balkanization.

Knowledge Feed — For Your Internship

Concept · Macro

The carry trade & why BI hikes when IDR weakens

A carry trade borrows in a low-yield currency (JPY, USD when rates were 0) and lends/invests in a high-yield currency (IDR, BRL, MXN). The trader earns the rate differential. When the high-yield currency weakens, carry positions lose money on FX faster than they earn on yield → traders unwind → currency weakens more.

BI hikes the BI Rate to widen the rate differential vs USD, restoring carry attractiveness. The cost: domestic loans get more expensive, equities sell off (which is exactly what IHSG just did).

Concept · Equity Research

How to structure a stock pitch (the 5-slide rule)

1. Investment thesis in 3 bullets — what's mispriced, why, catalyst.
2. Business overview — what they sell, to whom, moat.
3. Financial model summary — revenue drivers, margin path, FCF.
4. Valuation — comps + DCF + sum-of-parts where relevant. Show the bridge from current to target price.
5. Risks & what would change my mind — be specific (not "macro risk"; say "if BI hikes another 50bps, my DCF discount rate moves to X").

Senior bankers reject pitches that don't say "what would make me wrong."

Concept · M&A

Accretion / dilution math — the IB litmus test

You'll be asked this in 90% of M&A interviews. The mental model:

An acquisition is accretive if the acquirer's EPS rises post-deal, dilutive if it falls. Quick test: compare the target's earnings yield (E/P) to the after-tax cost of financing.

If target EY > after-tax cost of debt → debt-financed deal is accretive.
If target EY > acquirer's EY → all-stock deal is accretive.

That's it. The Excel model adds synergies and intangible amortization, but this is the back-of-envelope partners use.

Concept · IPO Mechanics

What "upsized at the high end" actually means

Quantinuum was "upsized at $53–55." Translation: the underwriter (lead bank) saw the order book oversubscribed at the original range, so they (a) raised the price range and/or (b) raised share count. Upsized + above range = strong demand signal. Day-1 pop above issue confirms it; trading below issue (a "break") means the deal got mispriced and the bank failed quality control.

For your pitch: cite the offering's reception as a sentiment indicator for the sector.

Concept · Private Equity

Why PE loves stable cash flow over growth

A leveraged buyout uses 50–70% debt. Debt service is fixed. If revenue grows but margins are volatile, you can miss an interest payment in a bad quarter → covenant breach → equity wiped. PE pays a premium for businesses with predictable, recurring revenue (SaaS, healthcare services, utilities-like infrastructure) because predictability lets you lever up safely. That's why Berkshire buying a homebuilder (cyclical!) is news — it implies they see cycle bottom and durable demand ahead.

Concept · M&A

EV/EBITDA — Why Bankers Use It

Enterprise Value (EV) = Market Cap + Total Debt − Cash + Minority Interest + Preferred Stock.

It's the price to buy the whole company, including paying off lenders. EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, D&A) is a proxy for operating cash flow.

Bankers use EV/EBITDA in M&A because it's capital-structure-neutral — two firms with identical operations but different debt loads will still be comparable. P/E doesn't do that (because interest expense distorts E).
EV / EBITDA = Total Enterprise Value ÷ EBITDA
Ingredion is buying Tate & Lyle for $3.6B (EV).
If T&L EBITDA is $400M, EV/EBITDA = 9.0x.
Compare vs sector median (say 11x) → Ingredion paid a discount, plausibly because of integration risk. That's a real pitch angle.
Concept · Macro

Why a Central Bank Hikes to Defend Currency

When BI hikes rates, IDR-denominated assets pay more carry for foreign investors. A US fund earning 4.6% on UST can now earn 5.5% on Indo bonds. That demand for Rupiah = currency appreciation (or at least stops the bleeding).

The cost: higher domestic borrowing rates slow growth. Consumer loans, mortgages, corporate capex all get repriced. So BI's surprise hike is a trade-off: FX stability now, growth drag later.
UIP: i_local ≈ i_foreign + expected FX depreciation
If US 10Y = 4.6% and IDR is expected to depreciate 2%, fair IDR yield = 6.6%. BI at 5.5% is still below "fair" — that's why the rupiah is at record lows despite the hike.
Concept · IPO

What "Book-Building" and "Oversubscription" Mean

In an IPO, the underwriter (Goldman, MS, JPM) collects bids from institutional investors over a roadshow period. Each investor signals how many shares they'd buy at what price. That's the book.

Oversubscription = total demand exceeds shares available. SpaceX is 3.5–4x oversubscribed → demand is $250B+ for $75B of supply.

Why it matters: oversubscription predicts first-day pop. Heavily oversubscribed deals usually rip on Day 1 because allocations were rationed.
For SpaceX at $135 issue price, with 3.5x demand, the Street consensus first-day open is in the $160–180 range. Use this framing when pitching IPO participation to a client — it's how the buy-side actually thinks.
Concept · Markets

The "Funding Source" Mechanic in Big IPOs

When a mega-IPO prices, institutional funds need cash on settlement day. They raise it by selling existing positions — often in the same sector. A SpaceX IPO this big could mean PMs sell Tesla, NVDA, Mag-7 to fund SPCX allocations.

This is why you sometimes see comparable names dip the day before an IPO. It's not fundamentals — it's plumbing.
Watch Tesla on June 11–12. If it dips 2–4% with no news, that's likely SPCX funding flow. Smart traders fade it. This is the kind of intuition that distinguishes a junior from a senior on the desk.

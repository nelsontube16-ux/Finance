§ 08 — STUDENT DESK

M&A · ACCRETION / DILUTION

Why the NextEra/Dominion deal is taught the day it's announced

Whenever a deal is announced as all-stock (like NEE/D), the very first question any banker, PE associate, or buy-side analyst asks is: "Is it accretive or dilutive to EPS in Year 1?" If you can answer this within five minutes on a napkin, you signal you're ready to be staffed on a live deal.

Pro-Forma EPS = (NI_Acquirer + NI_Target + After-Tax Synergies − After-Tax Cost of Financing) ÷ (Shares_Acquirer + New Shares Issued to Target) Accretion / Dilution = (Pro-Forma EPS − Standalone Acquirer EPS) ÷ Standalone Acquirer EPS

The fast intuition (no model needed):

  • In an all-stock deal, the deal is accretive when the acquirer's P/E > the target's P/E (you're buying earnings cheaper than yours).
  • In an all-cash deal financed with debt, it's accretive when the target's earnings yield (E/P) > the after-tax cost of debt.
  • Synergies always tilt accretive; integration costs tilt dilutive in Year 1.
Applied to NEE/D: NextEra typically trades at a regulated-utility-premium multiple (~22–24x fwd PE) while Dominion trades closer to ~17–19x. So on first read, the optical arithmetic is accretive for NEE before synergies. Now layer the $1–1.5B synergy run-rate guidance and the regulated rate-base growth from AI data center demand — the deal model writes itself. That is your pitch hook.

Drill for today: Pull NEE and D 10-Ks. Compute standalone EPS, exchange ratio implied by $66.8B, pro-forma EPS, accretion %. Send me the number — I'll mark it.

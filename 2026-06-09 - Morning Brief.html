Geopolitics — The Master Variable

2 min

If you only watch one driver this quarter, watch this. Everything downstream — Fed, EM, oil, equity multiples — bends to it.

Iran tensions re-escalate; Trump teases fresh strikes

Risk-Off Energy Source: TheStreet, Yahoo Finance

Equities sold off into the close after Trump announced the US would respond to an Iranian attack on a US military helicopter. The market action was clean: tech (-) + energy (-) led, defensives held — the textbook risk-off signature of a Middle East flare-up.

Brent crude is bid above $110 with the Strait of Hormuz tanker traffic still constrained (carries ~20% of global oil). The 2026 Iran war has now lasted ~3 months and shows no de-escalation path.

Why an analyst cares: Oil at $110+ is the input that breaks the disinflation story. Every model — Fed cut path, EM current accounts, S&P earnings — has to be rebuilt around a sustained energy shock. If you're writing a pitch, your base case must price this in, not treat it as a sidebar.
How it transmits — for the first-timer
Oil up → fuel, fertilizer, freight all cost more → headline inflation rises → central banks can't cut rates → cost of capital stays high → growth-stock valuations (which discount future cash flows) compress → tech sells off. Meanwhile, oil producers (XOM, CVX, MEDC.JK) see margins expand → energy stocks rally. That's why an "oil shock" looks like a sector rotation, not a market crash.

US Catalyst BLS · Jun 10, 08:30 ET

April CPI ran +0.6% MoM / +3.8% YoY; core stuck at 2.8% (vs 2.7% expected). Services ex-energy +3.3%, goods +1.1% — tariffs and oil are doing the work. Tomorrow's May print will decide whether the "patient pause" narrative survives or whether traders need to start pricing a hike, not a cut.

Trade setup: A hot print = USD ↑, bonds ↓, gold ↑, EM (incl. IHSG) ↓ hard. A cool print = relief rally in tech and EM. Polymarket has it at 99.1% no-cut at June 16–17 FOMC — meaning the surprise is asymmetric to the downside.

Bank Indonesia hikes 50bps to 5.25% — defending the rupiah

Indonesia Rates BI · Press release

First BI hike since April 2024. Inflation accelerated to 3.08% in May (vs 2.42% April). Rupiah at 18,039/USD — that's the weakest in a generation. BI is choosing currency stability over growth support, which is the right textbook answer but politically painful.

Read-through to IHSG: Higher rates = higher discount rate for Indonesian equities = lower fair-value multiples. Banks (BBCA, BMRI) get a NIM tailwind. Property (CTRA, PWON), small-caps, and high-multiple growth names get hit hardest.
The mechanism — central-bank trilemma
An emerging-market central bank can only ever pick two of three: (1) stable currency, (2) free capital flows, (3) independent monetary policy. Indonesia keeps capital flows open and floats the rupiah — so the moment the Fed pauses cuts and oil spikes, BI must hike to defend the IDR or watch it collapse. This is why EM rates are slaves to the Fed.

Today's Lesson — Sharpen the Sword

2 min

One new concept per day. Internalize it before market open. Today: Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) Valuation — the technique behind almost every conglomerate pitch.

Concept

What Sum-of-the-Parts means

When a company has multiple business lines that deserve different valuation multiples — like SpaceX (launch business + Starlink + future Mars optionality) — you can't just slap one EV/EBITDA on the whole thing. You value each segment separately, then add them up.

It's the cleanest way to argue a company is undervalued (or overvalued). If management's stock trades at $100, but the parts add up to $150 if spun off → that's your "hidden value" pitch.

Mechanism

How to actually do it — 4 steps

1. Split the company into reporting segments (from 10-K / annual report). E.g. Alphabet = Search + YouTube + Cloud + Other Bets.

2. Apply the right comp multiple to each. Search → mature ad business → use Meta/Baidu comps. Cloud → growth infra → use AMZN AWS or AZURE comps. Other Bets → pure optionality → DCF with high discount rate or zero it.

3. Sum the segment valuations → that's your gross enterprise value.

4. Subtract net debt, add back cash → divide by share count → that's your SOTP price target.

SOTP Equity Value = Σ (Segment EV) − Net Debt + Cash + Tax-adjusted Optionality
Where it shows up at work

Why this is non-negotiable for an analyst

Every activist short report (think Hindenburg) and every break-up pitch uses SOTP. Every IB MD will ask: "Run me a sum-of-the-parts on this conglomerate." If you can do it cold on a napkin in 4 minutes, you will stand out in any internship interview.

Today, try this exercise: pull up Astra International (ASII.JK) — it has automotive, financial services, agribusiness, infrastructure, heavy equipment, and IT all under one roof. Build a SOTP. You'll see why the holding-co discount exists.

Book of the Day

30 sec
The Intelligent Investor
— Benjamin Graham

The Intelligent Investor — Benjamin Graham

First published 1949 · Revised 2003 by Jason Zweig · ~640 pages · Bible of value investing

If you read one book through your internship, make it this one. Warren Buffett calls it "by far the best book on investing ever written." It teaches the two ideas that survive every crisis: (1) Mr. Market is a manic-depressive — your job is to use his moods, not catch them; (2) margin of safety — you don't invest unless price is sufficiently below intrinsic value to absorb being wrong.

Daily ritual (15 min): Read one Zweig commentary section per morning before market open. They are short, ground each Graham principle in a modern example, and prime your brain to think like an analyst, not a trader. Pair with today's brief = you'll be a different student in 90 days.
